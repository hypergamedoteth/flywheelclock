Approx. gold ever mined (USGS cumulative production)

  • USGS: about 187,000 t "historically produced" (the FAQ pairs this with 57,000 t of reserves, the MCS 2017 figure, so it's read here as through end-2016). 4
  • + world mine production 2017–2022: 3,260 + 3,310 + 3,260 + 3,050 + 3,120 + 3,160 = 19,160 t 3
  • + 2023–2025: 3,250 + 3,280 + 3,300 = 9,830 t 2 1
  • = ~215,990 t approx. gold ever mined
  • Cross-check: USGS year-by-year world production 1900–2025 alone sums to 185,173 t; the rest (~30,817 t) was mined before 1900.

Who owns it (the math)

  • Central banks & governments: 31,221.9 t, the sum of the latest monthly gold holdings of 79 reporters to the IMF (including the ECB). 5
  • IMF's own gold: 2,814.1 t (90.5M oz). 6
  • ETFs: GLD 1,041.65 + IAU 463.05 + GLDM 234.85 = 1,739.55 t, from each issuer's own published holdings. 7 9 8
  • Tether: 146.19 t backing USDT + 22.01 t backing XAU₮ = 168.2 t (Jun 30, 2026). 10
  • Disclosed individuals: none. No person's personal bullion holding is publicly verified.
  • Privately held, owner not public = 215,990 − 35,943.8 = 180,046.2 t (83.4%)

The "owner not public" bucket is everything without a named holder: jewelry, private bars and coins, gold in other ETFs and funds, industrial gold, gold lost over history, and any official gold not reported to the IMF. Dates differ by row. Treat all shares as approximate.

Jewelry is the largest single use of newly consumed gold, according to USGS's 2026 summary. 1

Fund shareholders are the beneficial owners. Brokers report fund positions in SEC 13F filings, but those name the broker, not the end investor, and 13Fs don't cover physical bullion. Billionaire gold positions that show up publicly (e.g. Tudor Investment) are fund positions, not personal metal.

Disclosed by others

  • IMF gold sits in depositories in New York, London, Paris and Nagpur; the split isn't public. 6

  • France: the 129 t once held in New York was sold and replaced in Europe. 30
  • India: the majority is now held domestically. 31

Still in the ground

World reserves: about 66,000 t (USGS). At 2025's pace that's about 20 years of mining (66,000 ÷ 3,300, derived). Reserves get revised up as prices rise and new deposits are proven, so this isn't a countdown. 1
